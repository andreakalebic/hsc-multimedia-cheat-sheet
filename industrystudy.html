Structural considerations — tested 2023, 2024

Organisation Types

Sole traderOne owner — all decisions, all profit, all liability. Simple structure, fast decisions. E.g. freelance videographer.
PartnershipTwo or more owners — shared decisions, profit, and liability. Formal agreement advisable. E.g. small production studio.
Company (Pty Ltd)Separate legal entity — shareholders own shares, directors manage. Limited liability protects personal assets.
CooperativeMember-owned and controlled — each member has equal say regardless of investment. Common in arts and media.

Management Structures

Hierarchical (tall)Many management levels — formal authority structure. Clear reporting lines but slow decision-making. Suits large organisations.
FlatFew management levels — fast decisions, more employee autonomy. Suits small creative businesses and startups.
Strategic levelCEO, board — sets long-term direction, major decisions, vision
Tactical levelManagers, department heads — implement strategy, manage teams and resources
Operational levelSupervisors, workers — day-to-day tasks, direct production work
Quality controlSystems ensuring consistent product quality — testing, auditing, standards compliance, inspection processes
Personnel issues — tested 2017, 2019, 2021

Industrial Relations

Industrial relationsThe relationship between employers, employees, and unions — governed by the Fair Work Act 2009
Fair Work Act 2009Federal legislation setting minimum wages, conditions, and dispute resolution processes for Australian workers
AwardLegal minimum pay and conditions set for an industry or occupation — e.g. Broadcasting, Recorded Entertainment and Cinemas Award
Enterprise agreementNegotiated agreement between a specific employer and their workers — must provide conditions at least equal to the relevant award
UnionOrganisation representing workers' collective interests in negotiating pay, conditions, and dispute resolution with employers
RedundancyThe position — not the person — is no longer required. Worker is entitled to a redundancy payment based on years of service.
RetrenchmentInvoluntary termination due to economic necessity — triggers legal obligations including notice periods and redundancy pay

Employment Law & Opportunity

EEOEqual Employment Opportunity — illegal to discriminate in hiring or promotion based on race, gender, age, disability, pregnancy, religion, or sexual orientation
Multi-skillingTraining workers in multiple roles and tasks — increases workforce flexibility and reduces vulnerability when staff are absent
UpskillingProviding additional training to existing employees to expand their capabilities — often in response to new technology
On-the-job trainingLearning while working in the actual role — cost-effective but requires experienced supervisors
Voluntary redundancyEmployer offers a package for workers to choose to leave — avoids forced retrenchment, reduces conflict
Cost/benefit analysisSystematic comparison of the financial and non-financial costs and benefits of a management decision before implementation
Environmental & sociological — tested most frequently

Environmental Impact

Sustainable developmentMeeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs — Brundtland Commission definition
E-wasteDiscarded electronic equipment (computers, screens, phones). Contains toxic materials. Must be recycled responsibly under the National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS).
Carbon footprintTotal greenhouse gas emissions produced by an individual, organisation, or product throughout its lifecycle
Take-back programsManufacturer-run schemes collecting end-of-life products for responsible recycling — required under Australian e-waste legislation
Green energyRenewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro) used to power data centres and production facilities — reduces carbon emissions
Circular economyEconomic model where materials are kept in use as long as possible — reduce, reuse, recycle, recover

Location & Sociological Factors

Land costsPremium inner-city locations provide better infrastructure and talent access but higher rent — outer areas offer lower costs but may have inferior data connectivity
Data infrastructureMultimedia businesses require high-speed broadband (NBN, fibre) — location must support fast data transfer for large file sharing and cloud services
Remote workCloud tools allow multimedia workers to collaborate from any location — reduces need for centralised offices but requires reliable internet access
LegislationLocal, state, and federal laws affecting operations — e-waste regulations, broadcasting licences, privacy laws, workplace laws
EISEnvironmental Impact Statement — required document assessing potential environmental effects before major new development or expansion
WHS — Work Health & Safety

Hierarchy of Controls

Remember the order — most to least effective

1. Elimination → 2. Substitution → 3. Engineering controls → 4. Administrative controls → 5. PPE

EliminationRemove the hazard entirely — safest option. E.g. don't film the dangerous stunt scene; use CGI instead.
SubstitutionReplace with something less hazardous. E.g. film on a closed replica set instead of a live road.
Engineering controlsPhysical barriers or devices. E.g. traffic controllers, barricades, safety railings around elevated camera positions.
Administrative controlsPolicies, procedures, training, signage. E.g. restrict filming to non-peak traffic times, safety briefings for all crew.
PPEPersonal Protective Equipment — last resort. Helmets, high-vis vests, hearing protection, safety footwear.

WHS Key Terms

HazardAnything with the potential to cause injury, illness, or damage — e.g. cables on a film set, heavy camera equipment
RiskThe likelihood AND consequence of a hazard causing harm — assessed as a combination of probability and severity
Risk assessmentSystematic process of identifying hazards, evaluating risks, and implementing controls — required before any filming activity
ErgonomicsDesigning workplaces and equipment to fit the human body — prevents RSI, back injury, and eye strain from prolonged screen use
WHS Act 2011 (NSW)Primary legislation — employers have a duty of care to ensure the health and safety of all workers. Breaches can result in heavy fines and criminal prosecution.
Safe Work AustraliaCommonwealth agency that sets national WHS policy, codes of practice, and model legislation
Historical developments — tested 2023, 2025

Key Historical Milestones in Multimedia

1970s–1980s

  • Personal computers emerge (Apple II, IBM PC)
  • Analogue video editing — linear tape-to-tape
  • First computer graphics in film (TRON, 1982)
  • MIDI standard introduced (1983)
  • CD-ROM introduced for data storage

1990s–2000s

  • World Wide Web goes public (1993)
  • Non-linear digital editing (Avid, Premiere)
  • CGI goes mainstream — Jurassic Park (1993)
  • MP3 format democratises music (late 1990s)
  • DVD replaces VHS — digital home video
  • Digital cameras replace film in broadcast

2010s–Present

  • Smartphones democratise content creation
  • Streaming replaces physical media (Netflix, Spotify)
  • Cloud storage and global collaboration
  • 4K and HDR become consumer standards
  • AI tools for content generation
  • Subscription software replaces perpetual licences
  • VR and AR enter consumer market
Sample questions — industry study
Short Answer5 marks
A multimedia company is planning to expand its operations. Describe the industrial relations issues that would occur as the company adapts to this change.
STEEL Format
S
Expanding a multimedia company creates a range of industrial relations issues involving the recruitment of new employees, updating of employment agreements, and compliance with equality legislation. 1 mark
T
Recruitment of new staff must comply with Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) legislation — the company cannot discriminate based on race, gender, age, or disability. All advertised positions must be open to suitable candidates on merit, and selection panels should be trained to avoid unconscious bias. 1 mark
E
Existing employees may need to renegotiate their enterprise agreements to reflect new roles, responsibilities, and remuneration — unions representing workers will likely be involved in these negotiations. The company must ensure all new and existing agreements meet or exceed the award minimum conditions under the Fair Work Act 2009. 1 mark
E
New technology introduced as part of the expansion may require existing employees to be upskilled or multi-skilled — industrial relations issues can arise if workers feel threatened by new equipment or if training requires overtime or changes to working conditions. 1 mark
L
Managing expansion effectively requires transparent communication with employees and unions throughout the process, ensuring workers understand the benefits of the change and have genuine input into how it is implemented — reducing resistance and protecting both the company's legal obligations and its workplace relationships. 1 mark
Extended Response10 marks
Evaluate the changes that have taken place in resource management in the multimedia industry, providing a clear determination of their value.
STEEL Format — aim for 5–6 distinct evaluative points
S
Resource management in the multimedia industry — encompassing equipment, personnel, software, administration, and facilities — has undergone transformative changes driven by digital technology, directly improving efficiency, reducing costs, and expanding creative capability. 1 mark
T
The shift from physical to digital production equipment has dramatically reduced capital costs. High-quality cameras, editing workstations, and audio interfaces that previously cost hundreds of thousands of dollars are now available for tens of thousands — or less — while delivering superior output. This has democratised professional multimedia production and allowed smaller businesses to compete. 1 mark
E
Software licensing models have fundamentally changed resource management. The industry has largely shifted from purchasing expensive perpetual licences (e.g. Adobe CS6 at $2,000+) to subscription models (Adobe Creative Cloud at ~$60/month). This eliminates large upfront capital expenditure, ensures teams always use the latest version, and allows businesses with intermittent software needs to subscribe only when required — saving significant money. 1 mark
E
Cloud computing has revolutionised storage and collaboration resource management. Previously, large video projects required expensive on-premises server infrastructure and physical hard drives. Cloud platforms allow projects to be stored remotely and accessed by geographically distributed teams simultaneously, reducing hardware costs and enabling flexible working arrangements. 1 mark
E
Administrative resource management has also been transformed — all invoicing, payroll, equipment auditing, material tracking, and scheduling functions that once required dedicated departments can now be handled by a single person using integrated management software (e.g. MYOB, Xero, project management platforms). This has produced significant labour cost savings and reduced administrative error. 1 mark
L
Overall, the value of these changes to resource management is very high — they have simultaneously lowered barriers to entry for new businesses, reduced operating costs for established firms, improved the quality and speed of production, and enabled a more flexible, globally distributed workforce. The multimedia industry today achieves greater output at lower cost than at any previous point in its history, primarily as a result of these resource management transformations. 2 marks — evaluative conclusion
